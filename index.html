Bearing fault diagnosis from raw vibration

A 1D-CNN and a feature-based gradient-boosting model trained on the Case Western Reserve University bearing data, evaluated without window leakage (train on motor loads 0–2 hp, test on the unseen 3 hp recordings). The CNN below runs entirely in your browser via onnxruntime-web — no server.

Method

  1. Data: 40 CWRU recordings, 12 kHz drive-end accelerometer — normal + inner race / ball / outer race (centred) faults at 0.007, 0.014, 0.021 in, each at 0/1/2/3 hp. First 121k samples per recording, 2048-sample windows, 50 % overlap.
  2. Features (GBM / RF): 12 time-domain stats (RMS, kurtosis, crest factor, …), 16 FFT band energies + spectral shape, envelope-spectrum band energies and peak amplitudes at BPFI/BPFO/BSF/FTF harmonics (Hilbert envelope of the 2–5.5 kHz resonance band).
  3. 1D-CNN: 4 conv blocks with a wide 64-tap first kernel, global average pooling; per-window z-score input; trained 15 epochs on CPU, exported to ONNX.
  4. Evaluation: random window split (leaky, for reference), leave-one-load-out (4 folds), and a cross-fault-size test (train 7 & 21 mil, test 14 mil).
  5. Health index: Mahalanobis distance of the feature vector from healthy-only training data; alarm at the 99th percentile of healthy windows.

Serving: FastAPI /predict (JSON window) and /predict/csv, /health; this page doubles as the dashboard.
